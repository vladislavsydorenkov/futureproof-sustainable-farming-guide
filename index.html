Farming through war.
Innovating against the odds.

Ukraine's agricultural research system is under extraordinary pressure. This project puts practical experimentation back in the field, turns evidence into decisions farmers can use, and creates a pathway from research plots to commercial farms.

From plots to practice

Research farmers can actually use.

The work tests reduced tillage, biological products, biochar and approaches to restoring war-damaged soils, then translates the evidence into practical guidance, training and farm-level experimentation.

01

Reduced tillage

Testing whether farmers can cut fuel, field passes and soil disturbance without sacrificing the crop.

02

Biological products

Measuring yield and grain-quality responses and where biological inputs add practical value.

03

Biochar

Testing moisture retention, soil effects and crop response under Ukrainian field conditions.

04

War-damaged soils

Exploring practical routes to restore soil function where war has disrupted productive land.

Strongest observed barley treatment

The combination of biochar + biologicals produced the highest observed bunker yield and raised protein from 11.2% in the control to 14.0%. These yield and protein observations were not replicated, so they should be read as experimental signals rather than statistically proven effects.

2026 field evidence was generated with UkrNDIPVT and Mykolaiv National Agrarian University. The project turns that evidence into farmer-facing guidance and training, with a proposed 2027 Adoption Challenge intended to test whether promising practices can move from research into routine farm decisions.
