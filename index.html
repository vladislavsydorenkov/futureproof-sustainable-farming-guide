Farming through war.

Ukraine's agricultural research system is under extraordinary pressure. This project puts practical experimentation back in the field, turns evidence into decisions farmers can use, and creates a pathway from research plots to commercial farms.

Farmer economics · what changes in the cost base

Less diesel. Fewer passes.
More margin left on the farm.

The strongest economic result from the 2026 trials was not a dramatic yield jump. It was a lower cost of producing each hectare. Reduced tillage cut fuel, machinery and labour costs enough to improve margins even where yields were slightly lower.

What the cost reduction did to profit

Additional profit versus conventional ploughing, after accounting for the observed yield differences. This is the part that matters commercially: lower costs translated into a stronger per-hectare margin.

Despite a small yield difference, the lower cost base lifted profit.

A useful risk lens: for spring wheat, the UAH 3,824/ha cost saving gave domestic Mini-till a break-even cushion of about 0.55 t/ha at the trial's assumed grain price. In other words, conventional ploughing would need to produce roughly 550 kg/ha more wheat just to offset its higher cost.
